# The FPS kit

A first-person shooter to start from, in a project that is yours to change: a yard and a house of two rooms (`scenes/main`), Bravo (1.8 m) or Brava (1.7 m) in first person with a rifle and a shotgun, interaction (the `interact` control, E: doors, a drawer, pickups, a socket), cube enemies and waves, a HUD, and every control an action the player can change (a pad works too). Everything is plain scripts and prefabs: read them, change them, delete what you do not want. Read `AGENTS.md` and the topic files in `docs/` for how the engine works; this file is about what this template made of it.

## Play it
- The controls the kit starts with (each is an **action**, see Controls below; the player changes them in the pause menu's **Controls** screen): **WASD** move, **Shift** run, **Ctrl** crouch, **Space** jump, **mouse** look (click once to lock the pointer), **left mouse** fire, **right mouse** aim (hold), **R** reload, **E** use what the crosshair is on, **F** the flashlight (on and off, with whatever is in hand), **1** to **9** take the gear of that slot in hand (the control of the one in hand puts it away, again takes it out), the **mouse wheel** goes through the weapons, **Tab** the Journal, **Esc** the pause menu, **F5** and **F9** quick save and load. A gamepad (Xbox layout) has its own: left stick move, right stick look, **A** jump, **B** crouch, **X** reload, **Y** use, **R3** (the right stick, pressed) the flashlight, **RT** fire, **LT** aim, **RB** and **LB** next and previous weapon, the D-pad the first four slots, **Start** pause, **Back** the Journal.
- Walk into the house: the front door opens with the interact control (E); the inner door is locked, its key is in the drawer of the cabinet in the first room; the power cell on the table goes into the socket in the back room (it switches the lamp on). The amber pad in the yard starts the waves.
- The weapon is carried **relaxed** (low) until you shoot, aim or reload, then **alert** (at the shoulder) and it goes back to relaxed after `Alert Seconds` (15) without a shot (at once when the weapon is put away, or a grenade or a medkit is in hand; changing one firearm for another keeps it alert); running lowers it too. That is how the character holds it. **Aiming** (right mouse held) is something more: the weapon comes to the eye, the field of view narrows and the spread closes; a sprint, a reload or a change of weapon lets it go.
- Four things in hand: the **rifle** (slot 1: automatic, 30 rounds), the **shotgun** (slot 2: ten pellets a shot, one shot at a time, six shells, a pump sound), a **grenade** (slot 3: the left mouse throws it) and a **medkit** (slot 4: the left mouse held heals). Each firearm keeps its own rounds when another is in hand; the grenades and the medkits are counted by the Inventory, and two of the first and one of the second are on the ground near the start.

## Controls
Nothing in the kit reads a key: every control is an **action** (a name the scripts ask for, with the keys, mouse buttons and pad buttons it answers to), declared in `project.json` under `input` (in the editor: Project Settings > Input) and used as `Input.action('interact').down(callback)` (`docs/input.md`). The engine keeps what the player changes (in its own room of the game's Vault: the kit writes no code for it), and a default fixed later still reaches every player who never moved that control.

| Action | Keyboard and mouse | Gamepad | Read by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `move`, `look` | WASD, the mouse | left stick, right stick | `PlayerMovement`, `FirstPersonCamera` (the engine's own actions) |
| `jump`, `run`, `crouch` | Space, Left Shift, Left Ctrl | A, L3, B | `PlayerMovement` (engine's own) |
| `fire`, `aim` | left mouse, right mouse | RT, LT | `Loadout`, which passes them to the gear in hand |
| `reload` | R | X | `Loadout` |
| `next-weapon`, `previous-weapon` | wheel down, wheel up | RB, LB | `Loadout` |
| `slot-1` to `slot-9` | 1 to 9 | D-pad up, right, down, left (slots 1 to 4) | `Loadout` (`FirstPersonArms` for `slot-1`, only when its Equip Key is on) |
| `interact` | E | Y | `Interactor` |
| `flashlight` | F | R3 | `Flashlight` |
| `pause` | Esc | Start | `PauseMenu`, `Journal` |
| `journal` | Tab | Back | `Journal` |
| `quick-save`, `quick-load` | F5, F9 | none | `SaveGame` |

- **No control is on two actions** in the same family (a key, a mouse button or a pad button): the engine swaps the two when the player picks one that is used. `zoom` (an engine action only the third person camera reads) has no controls in this kit, so the wheel and the D-pad are free.
- **A new action**: add it in Project Settings > Input (a name, a type, the bindings of both families, none that another action has), write its words under `input.actions.<name>` in `en.lang` and `pt-BR.lang` (the Controls screen and the prompts use them), ask for it in a script (the `Flashlight` does: `Input.action('flashlight').down(...)`, removed in `onDestroy()`) and run: the Controls screen lists it under Other, or add its name to a group of `GROUPS` in `ui/hud.js`.
- **The Controls screen** (the pause menu's **Controls** button, `ui/index.html` and `ui/hud.js`): the Keyboard & Mouse and Gamepad tabs (it opens on the one the player is using), a row for each action with two places, and `move` shows its four directions. Click a place, press the new key, turn the wheel or press the pad button (a mouse button cannot be given from here: the page forwards a click on its empty space to the game as a mouse press, so while a place waits a catcher under the panel takes the clicks, and one cancels the wait and says nothing was changed; **Esc** cancels as well: the engine takes the keyboard for the wait and silences the actions, so that Esc does not close the menu; a wait that is still under way when the menu closes is ended with it); a control that was on another action swaps with it and the screen says so. A project can still give a mouse button to an action in `project.json` (Project Settings > Input). **Reset to defaults** asks twice. The actions that are an amount of change (`look`, `zoom`) and the sticks are shown, not changed. Every word is a `controls.*` entry of the language files.
- **What the HUD shows**: the key chip of the prompt and of the markers (`interact`), the number of each weapon (`slot-N`), the Journal's chip and the hint lines of the menus are written from the action the player has now (`data-input-action` in `ui/index.html`, `Input.actions.labelOf()` in `ui/hud.js`), in the device the player used last: a pad button is a round chip with the button's own name (`A`, `RT`, `LB`), drawn by `ui/hud.css` from `data-input-showing`, `data-input-button` and `data-input-style`. A weapon has no number chip on a pad for a slot with no pad button.
- **A key the page hears**: a page that holds the keyboard (a menu is open and something in it was clicked) hears keys the game does not, and only Esc is forwarded. The page asks `Input.actions.matches('journal', event)` (and `'pause'`) whether the key it heard is the one the player has given them, so those still close the Journal or the menu from there; no key is written in the page.

## How the parts talk
- **The `Hud` service** (`services/hud.service.ts`) is the only way the game puts something on the screen: the prompt line, ammo, health, stance, a message, notices of what was picked up or spent (`+ Key`, `-1 Power cell`: `notify()`, which the `Inventory` calls for every kind that is not hidden; the page keeps three at most at the corner over the health panel, one for each kind, each for `Notice Seconds` of the `JournalMenu`, 4; 0 keeps one until newer ones push it out; the whole list is in the Journal, under Items), a hit marker, which side a blow came from (`notifyDamaged(amount, angle)`: a red arc on that side of the crosshair), a crosshair that turns red over a living target (`setTarget`: the `Weapon` asks every frame with the same ray a shot uses, and a weapon with several pellets also along a ring inside its cone, so red means a shot now could hurt it) and markers over the things that can be used (`setMarkers`: the `Interactor` projects them to the screen, to a hundred thousandth of the window, and every reading carries the time of its frame, so that the page places each marker by its motion on every frame the screen draws: a label that is put where the last reading says trembles when the camera turns). Scripts `@Inject(Hud)` and call `setPrompt`, `setAmmo`, `showMessage`...; `ui/hud.js` reads it through `remote('Hud')`. Change the page to change the look; add a field to `IHudState` to show something new.
- **The `Inventory` service** keeps what the player carries by item id (`'key'`, `'power-cell'`, `'credits'`, and the rounds: `'ammo-rifle'`, `'ammo-shotgun'`). A `Pickup` adds, a `Socket` removes, a locked `Door` asks (and takes the key out when it opens, unless its `Use Up Key` is off: the Journal still lists it, as used), a `Firearm` draws its reserve from its `Ammo Type`. A kind may have a **limit** (the most that can be carried; 0 or none is no limit, as keys or credits have) and may be **hidden** from the lists the HUD and the Journal show (the rounds are in the ammo counter, the grenades and the medkits in the list of gear). Whoever knows a kind says what it is with `define(id, { name, description, limit, hidden, start })`: a weapon for its rounds, a `Pickup` for its item (when the scene starts, so a lock can name an item the player does not have yet, and the Journal can describe it); `add()` stops at the limit and `room(id)` says how many more fit. A `Pickup` that finds the player full stays where it is, and one that fits only some gives what fits. It tells the `Hud` and it is saved with the game.
- **The player's own pieces, by job**: `Health` (the number), `DamageFeedback` (the health bar, the side a blow came from, the hurt cry: it only listens to the `Health`), `Respawn` (what happens at zero: the message, the seconds, back to the start with everything full again: a game with lives changes `respawn()` only), `Loadout` (what is in hand) and `Weapon` (the firearm). The player's sounds are all below its `Sounds` entity: the shots, the reloads, the hurt cry and `Footstep Sounds`.
- **`Health` and `Hitbox`**: a shot hurts the `Health` it finds above the collider it hit, by damage times the multiplier of the `Hitbox` on that collider (head 2.5, body 1...). Anything can have a `Health`: the player, an enemy, a crate.
- **The Director** can drive the interactables: `Door`, `Socket` and `Pickup` have `@State()` booleans and `@Callable()` methods (`docs/director.md`).

## Make it yours
- **The Journal** (`Journal` service, `JournalMenu`, `Collectible`, `Objective`, and its page in `ui/`): what the player has found out, to look at again whenever it likes, with its `journal` action (**Tab**, a pad's Back): Objectives (they pile up: the latest is on top), Items (everything the player carries, with how many and what the `Pickup`'s `Description` says: only a view of the Inventory, so the whole list is always there to look at), Access (keys and cards, and whether they were used), Passwords, Notes and Audio Logs. An item the Journal has an entry for (an access) stays in its own category, and what the Inventory hides (the ammo, the grenades) is not listed. It takes the whole screen (the pause menu is never seen under it). The chip in the corner of the screen (the key and the menu's name) is not always there: it fades in when something new goes into the menu and out again after `Hint Seconds` (5; 0 keeps it on), set on the `JournalMenu`. The menu has the categories on the left, the entries of one in the middle with a drawing for the chosen one, and its text on the right (the note to read, or a play button for an audio log, which goes on playing while the game is paused because it does not go through an Audio Source). The `journal` action opens and closes it (the `pause` action closes it too), the pointer is let go while it is open and held again when it closes (Esc is not a gesture to a browser, so after it the next click holds the pointer). A `JournalMenu` in the scene says what the menu is called (`Title`: Journal here; Journal, Datapad, Notebook or Terminal in another game, with nothing else changing) and whether it pauses the game (`Pauses Game`, on). A `Collectible` is a thing to take (`Category`: notes, audio logs or passwords; `Title`, `Text`, `Audio Log`, `Open On Take`); a `Pickup` with `Journal Category` `access` lists its item under Access and the `Door` or the `Socket` that uses it marks it used; an `Objective` is a goal that a `Socket` completes (`Completes Objective`) or a script (`give()`, `complete()`, `fail()`). The yard has a torn page near the start, the house an audio log and a password and the objective of the power. A script uses the service: `journal.add({ id, category, title, body })`. `tools/fps-kit/add-journal.py` gives a project the same.
- **A weapon** is an entity under `Weapons` in the right hand (a child of the hand bone) with a `Firearm`: its numbers are the fields of that `Firearm`: `Name`, `Slot` (which `slot-N` action takes it in hand), `Automatic`, `Rounds per Minute`, `Pellets` (bullets in a shot: 1 a rifle, 8 to 12 a shotgun, each in a direction of its own inside the cone), `Damage` (of each), `Range`, the spread `Still / Moving / In the Air`, the `Crosshair` (Dot, Arcs: a ring of four arcs round a dot, or Twin Arcs, the shotgun's: two arcs one each side of a dot; all open with the spread, so the arcs show where the pellets may go), `Ammo Type` (the Inventory item it fires: weapons of one type share the rounds), `Magazine`, `Reserve` (what the player starts with, once per game and again after a respawn), `Reserve Limit` (the most of that type the player can carry; 0 is no limit), `Reload Time`, `Camera Kick`, and the `Aim` group (`Aim Field of View`, `Aim Time`, `Aim Spread`, and `Aim Side / Lower / Back / Tilt`: how far the weapon moves and how far its barrel is raised to end on the line of the eye, a little under it). Its parts are children: the model, `Muzzle` (a light and a flash, off) and `Grip Left` (where the left hand goes), and the sounds are references to entities under the player's `Sounds`. The `Weapon` on the player (one for all of them) has `Auto Reload` (on: a magazine that runs empty is reloaded by itself, once the shot is over, when there are rounds in reserve; a weapon taken in hand empty or given rounds while empty does the same; off: R reloads and the trigger on an empty magazine clicks). The two stances' poses are `WeaponStance` fields (`Lower`, `Back`, `Side`, `Tilt`...). The rifle and the shotgun are placeholders made of cubes: put a model in place of the cubes (keep `Muzzle` at the barrel's tip and `Grip Left` where the left hand goes).
- **What a bullet does** (`Weapon.onBulletHit()`, the numbers are the `Firearm`'s `Ballistics` group): a shot is a ray along the line of sight; with a `Muzzle Velocity` above 0 it is a curve that gravity bends (cast in pieces of 10 m, the world's gravity: 900 m/s for the rifle makes a drop of a few centimetres at 100 m, a slow bullet shows it) and the shot has no travel time. A bullet goes on after a hit and is cast again from where it came out (up to four hits): through a body it keeps `0.4` times its `Penetration` of its damage, and a body with several colliders is hurt once by one bullet; through a wall, a crate or a door it goes if the physics material of the collider is in the table `BULLET_SURFACES` of `weapons/bullet-surfaces.ts` (by the name of the `.pmat`: plaster 0.3 m, wood 0.2, cardboard 0.6, glass 0.06, concrete 0.08, steel and a collider with no physics material stop it): the thickness is measured by a second ray from just inside the wall that meets its far side, the damage left is `1 - thickness / (that number times Penetration)`, and the wall gets a mark on each side. `Penetration` multiplies it all: 1 for the rifle, 0.3 for a shotgun pellet (it passes thin plaster, never wood 0.1), 0 goes through nothing. The combat demo has a `Penetration Range` (six thin walls of other materials with a target dummy behind each, west of the start: plaster 0.05, plaster 0.2, wood 0.1, glass 0.03, concrete 0.05, steel 0.02) to try it on. `tools/fps-kit/add-ballistics.py` gives a project the numbers and the range.
- **The `Loadout` and the gear**: the `Loadout` on the player is what the player carries in hand: the entity in the right hand (`Gear`, the `Weapons` entity) with a `Gear` on each child (a `Firearm`, a `Throwable`, a `Medkit`...). It takes one in hand with the actions `slot-1` to `slot-9` (a gear's `Slot`; the keys 1 to 9 by default) and `next-weapon` / `previous-weapon` (the wheel, the shoulder buttons), puts it away with the slot of the one in hand, lists them on the HUD, points the left hand at the grip of the one in hand, and passes `fire`, `aim` and `reload` (the left mouse, the right mouse and **R** by default; the triggers and **X** on a pad) on to it (the gear's hooks `onPrimaryDown()`, `onSecondaryDown()`, `onReload()`..., and the signals `onPrimaryDown`, `onSecondaryDown`, `onReload`, `onSwitch` for anything else that listens: the `Weapon` fires a `Firearm` from them). `Start Equipped` (off: the first gear starts put away, loaded, and its slot brings it up) and `Fire Without Pointer Lock` (a test's) are its fields. `Weapon` is only for firearms: with a grenade in hand it does nothing.
- **The grenade** (`Throwable`, slot 3): the left mouse winds up (`Wind-up`) and throws; one is taken off the count, the `Projectile` prefab (`prefabs/gear/grenade.fab`: a Rigid Body, a Collider and a `Grenade`) is made in front of the eyes and sent where the player looks, `Lift` degrees above it, at `Throw Speed` plus the body's own. The `Grenade` goes off after `Fuse` seconds: the engine's Small explosion, and every `Health` inside `Radius` takes `Damage` falling off with the distance (a wall between the blast and a body protects it); a rigid body is pushed. The count is the Inventory's (`Item Id` `grenade`, `Start With`, `Limit`): a `Pickup` of that id gives more, and the list on the HUD says how many are left (`Grenade ×2`). The throw is heard from the player (`Throw Sound`, an entity under the player's `Sounds`) and the blast from where it happens (`Blast Sound` of the `Grenade`: the grenade is gone in the same frame, so an entity of its own plays it and is taken away when it has ended); the medkit plays its `Use Sound` when it has healed. The three sounds are placeholders made by `tools/sounds/combat.py` (`grenade-throw`, `explosion`, `medkit-use`): drop a better file over the same name; `tools/fps-kit/add-gear-sounds.py` wires them into a project that has none.
- **The medkit** (`Medkit`, slot 4): the left mouse HELD heals `Amount` over `Use Time` and spends one (`Item Id` `medkit`); letting go, taking another gear or dying stops it with nothing spent, and a player at full health is told so. Placeholders like the rifle, the shotgun and the grenade (primitives: cubes, cylinders, spheres): put your own models in, keep `Grip Left` where the left hand goes.
- **The flashlight** (`Flashlight` on the player, the `flashlight` action: **F**, **R3** on a pad): not a gear, so it is not in the carousel and it never takes the weapon's place: the action switches the light on and off with the rifle, the grenade or nothing in hand. The light is the entity `Flashlight` under the `Eyes`: a Spot light (Intensity 6, Range 28, Outer Angle 28, Inner Angle 14, Shadow Type Soft, **Cache Shadows off**: it moves with the camera every frame), 30 cm ahead of the camera and 14 cm to its left (the weapon is on the right and would shadow it), shining along its own -Z, which is the camera's: it goes wherever the player looks, with no constraint. Whether it is lit is that entity's own Active (untick it in the scene to start lit; it starts dark, and a save keeps it), and the action does nothing while the player is dead or the game is not running. The `Flashlight` script's one field, `Beam`, says which entity is the light; change the light's numbers on the entity, or move it. The cone shows in the air when the main camera's stack has a **Volumetric Light** effect (add one to the camera's `.pps`: Add -> Select Effect; the light's own Volumetric > Scattering, 1, scales it, 0 hides it); without the effect it still lights what it hits. `tools/fps-kit/add-flashlight.py` gives a project the entity, the script, the action and its words.
- **A new kind of gear** (a flare, a radio: what the hand holds; the flashlight is not one, it is a switch on its own action): a script that `extends Gear` (`label`, `slot`, `gripLeft`) and overrides the hooks it uses: `onPrimaryDown()`, `onPrimaryUp()`, `onSecondaryDown()`, `onSecondaryUp()`, `onReload()`, `onBrought()`, `onPutAway()`, `showHud()` (what the HUD says of it), `prepare()` (tell the Inventory what its items are), `refill()` (the player is back from the dead) and `caption` (what the list says). It sits on an entity under `Weapons`, so it is on only while it is in hand: its `onUpdate()` runs only then.
- **A new weapon**: copy the `Shotgun` entity under `Weapons` (or the `Rifle`), give its `Firearm` the next free `Slot`, other numbers and another model; move its `Grip Left` and its `Muzzle`. The slot action, the wheel, the HUD list and the left hand's reach find it by themselves (it is whatever is under `Weapons` with a `Gear`). The model sits in the hand where you put the entity: the right hand holds the pistol grip at the entity's origin.
- **A new thing to use with `interact`** (E, a pad's Y): a script that `extends Interactable` (`prompt`, `usable`, `onUse(interactor)`), on an entity with a collider; `Door`, `Pickup` and `Socket` are the examples. Its `Marker` (Line: a glass box with the prompt over it, tied to it by a line; Simple: the box on it; None) and `Marker Height` say how it is shown on the screen, and the `Interactor`'s `Marker Range` and `Max Markers` say how many are drawn: the one looked at is the box, the near ones are a small mark. A door that slides (a drawer, a shutter) is the same `Door` with `Mode` Slide. A **double door** is the same `Door` with a `Second Leaf`: the other half of the pair, moved by the same progress and mirrored (a hinged leaf turns the opposite way, a sliding one slides the opposite way, so the two part), so both open and close together, with one prompt, one lock, one key and one state for a Director, and looking at either leaf and pressing E moves both. Give both leaves the same parent, each with its origin at its own hinge (or where it starts to slide from); `Amount` and `Axis` are the same for the two. A hinged door says who opens it with `Opens From`: `Either side, one swing` (the default: it always swings the way `Amount` says, so from the far side it swings towards the player), `Either side, away from the player` (it swings away from whoever opens it, from both sides) or `Front side only` (only from the side it swings away from; the other side is refused with `Wrong Side Prompt` and `Wrong Side Message`); the sides are measured from the hinge and the place of the panel, so the leaf needs a mesh.
- **Aiming at small things does not have to be exact**: an `Interactable` has an `Aim Radius` (metres; 0: only a look that lands on its collider counts, as a door or a supply). `Pickup` and `Collectible` start at 0.15, so their scenes need no change. The `Interactor` decides what is looked at in this order, and the first rule keeps the aim precise: (1) what the crosshair is on wins; (2) where it is on nothing to use (the floor beside a key), a thing whose centre the ray passes within its `Aim Radius` of is looked at, the one the aim is deepest in when two are, and never one behind a wall; (3) a thing looked at through its radius is held for the Interactor's `Aim Hold` (0.2 s) after the aim left it, and for as long as the aim is within one and a half radii, so a shaking hand does not make its box blink. E uses what is looked at, so the box that is shown is what E does. Separately, the mark nearest to where the player looks, within `Peek Angle` (12 degrees), says the name of its thing beside it (`peekName`: a pickup's item, a note's title; a door or a socket has none), so a small thing can be told before it is aimed at exactly. A thing may also have a box where the aim counts (`aimBox`, empty in `Interactable`, counted like rule 2): a **sliding `Door`** has its doorway, the place the leaf shuts (the place both shut, in a double door), so an open one is shut by looking anywhere in the doorway and not only at the sliver of the leaf that is left; its leaf needs a collider that is on. The small mark is for what the look can land on (a solid collider, an `Aim Radius` or a box) and for what has something to find: an open sliding door has none (`marked`), and a `Door` on an entity with no collider that is on has neither box nor mark.
- **A thing that only says what it is** (a painting, a sign, a monument): add the `Caption` script to its entity (it needs a collider; the model can be its children) and write its Caption Title and Caption Text (both may hold `{{ key }}`). Looking at it, the HUD shows the same glass card a pickup has, over the crosshair, with no key and no marker, and E on it does nothing. It is read from further away than things are used: `Look Distance` of the `Interactor` (6 m; `Reach`, 2.5 m, is still how far a thing is used). `Caption` is an `Interactable` with no prompt, so any `Interactable` whose `Prompt` is empty and that has a caption does the same (`describesOnly`).
- **A door has a name or a number**: a sign is a Text decal on the wall beside the door or on its frame (never on the `Leaf`: paint stays where it was laid and a door that moves slides out from under it), and the name or number is its `Text` (`A-03`, `Archive`, or a `{{ key }}`, read in the player's language and followed when the language changes). Put the decal in the door's prefab and change its `Text` in each instance: the editor marks it and the scene keeps it, while the decal and the rest are the prefab's (it needs the editor with prefab overrides). The `Door` script knows nothing about it; a script that wants another text sets `text` on the decal.
- **A lock says what is missing**: the prompt of a locked `Door` that has a `Key Item` is `Needs Prompt` ("Needs key"), and a `Socket` says `Needs Prompt` too ("Needs power cell"), with the name the `Pickup` gave the item. A lock may ask for several things: the ids separated by commas in `Key Item` or `Item Id` (`piece-1, piece-2, piece-3, piece-4, piece-5`) or `fuse x3` for three of one kind. The prompt then says which is next and how far the player is ("Needs Piece 3 (2 of 5)"), a door opens when the player carries all of them, and a socket takes what the player carries of what is missing each time (E again for the rest) and is filled, with the door it unlocks, when the last is in. The main level has the example: five data fragments (`Pickup`s with ids `fragment-1` to `fragment-5`: on the table and the cabinet of the first room, on the floor of both rooms and one in the yard) and the **Archive Console** in the back room, a `Socket` that asks for all five, lights its screen and a lamp when the last goes in and completes the objective "Restore the archive"; the Journal lists the fragments under Items while they are carried. `tools/fps-kit/add-archive.py` gives a project the same. A `Socket` with several pieces can show them going in: its `Placed Parts` is an entity (off at the start) whose children, all off, stand for the pieces one each in the order of `Item Id` (`fuse x3` is three in a row); it is switched on with the first piece and each child as its piece goes in (which are on is worked out from what is in, so a load shows the same ones). `Placed Object` still comes on when the last piece is in, and a socket with no `Placed Parts` shows nothing until then. The archive console has five recesses and five stand-ins; `tools/fps-kit/add-socket-partials.py` gives a project the same.
- **Which fields can hold a key**: every text the player reads: the `Prompt`, `Caption Title` and `Caption Text` of an Interactable, the prompts and messages of a `Door`, the prompts and messages of a `Socket`, a `Pickup`'s `Item Name`, `Message` and `Description`, a `Supply`'s messages, a Journal entry's, an Objective's and a Collectible's texts, a gear's `Name`, the `Text` of a Text decal. They accept `{{ key }}` and offer the keys as you type `{{` (the Inspector's autocomplete). **The kit's own texts are keys already**: the defaults of `Door`, `Socket`, `Pickup`, `Collectible` and the supplies (`{{ prompts.door.open }}`, `{{ prompts.take }}`, `{{ messages.picked }}`), the names, descriptions and journal texts of the main level (`{{ items.medkit.name }}`, `{{ objectives.power.title }}`, `{{ notes.page.body }}`) and the gear names (`{{ gear.rifle }}`). An entry that says `Take {item}` is read first and `{item}`, `{amount}`, `{have}` and `{need}` are filled after, so each language puts the words in its own order (`Pegar {item}`), and a name that is itself an entry (`{{ items.medkit.name }}`) is read before it goes in. The page draws again when the language changes. A new label: write it in the HTML with `data-translate`, add the entry to every `.lang`. A new language: add `es.lang` (New Language in the Assets panel) and it is in the pause menu's Language list; the player's choice is kept by the `PauseMenu` service (`settings.language`), and with none the game uses what the system asks for when it has it.

Your own kind overrides `get caption()`.

The pause menu's language list waits for the voices of the new language (`PauseMenu.setLanguage()` awaits `AudioSource.whenLanguageReady()`, `state.languageBusy`: the list and Continue are off and the row says Loading) before it gives the game back.

The entity has a trigger Collider (the space round it, for `Walk Over`) and an `AmmoSupply` (`Rounds`, `Ammo Type`: the item it puts in the Inventory, the `Ammo Type` of the weapons that fire it) or a `HealthSupply` (`Amount`), its drawing as children with a solid Collider on `Visual` (what the player's ray hits: a ray goes through triggers), `Visual` pointing at it (hidden while it is used up), an Audio Source on the entity for the sound, `Prompt` (`{amount}` is what it gives), `Message`, `Full Message` (what the use says when the weapon or the player has no use for it: `Ammo full`, `Health full`), `Respawn After` (0: taken for good) and `Walk Over` (on: it is also taken by walking into the trigger). - **The humanoid enemy** (`prefabs/enemies/enemy-humanoid.fab`, two in the yard and two in the combat demo): Bravo with the red materials (`bravo-hostile.mat`), the player's own walking and running clips (its `Animator` and `Character Animation` on the root, told by the way the `Nav Agent` moves it), and the same `EnemyCube` mind as the cube: it hunts, hits and falls the same way. A shot meets a hit zone on every bone that matters (head 2.5x, chest and belly 1x, pelvis 0.9x, arms 0.5 to 0.6x, legs 0.6 to 0.7x), each a `Hitbox` with **Follow Animation** on (a collider is placed once, so it is put where its bone is at every frame: `Collider.syncToEntity()`). Its capsule only walks: it is on the layer `Enemy Shell`, which the weapon's rays pass, so that a shot at the head is a head and not the capsule. To make another humanoid, copy the prefab and change the materials; a different skeleton needs the zones on its own bones.
- **Footsteps** (`Footsteps`, `scripts/locomotion/`, on the player and on the humanoid): one of four step sounds plays for every foot that comes down: at the point where the foot gets farthest ahead of the body along the way it goes (a heel strike, read from the ankle bones frame by frame, so any clip works: walk, run, crouch, sidestep, and no clip needs an animation event), louder with the speed (`Volume`, `Run Speed`, `Walk Share`), softer crouched (`Crouch Share`), silent standing, turning on the spot or in the air, and `Land Sound` when the body lands from a fall. The player's sounds are flat, the humanoid's come from where it is. Which variation of a holder plays is `Step Order`: **Pattern** (the default) plays them in the order of `Step Pattern`, `1 2 3 4 3 4 2 3` (each number is the place of a variation among the children of the holder, so four recordings make a walk of eight steps that repeats a sound now and then and never twice in a row; the counter is the character's own, one for both feet and for walking and running, and it starts again from the first place when the ground changes); **Sequence** plays them in the order of the children (drag them in the Hierarchy to change it) and starts again, each surface with its own place in it; **Random** never repeats the one that just played. A plain random choice plays the same sound twice in a row now and then, and that is what makes a walk sound like a machine. `Contact Height` is measured from the entity's origin (a level with steps may need a higher one). **Walking backwards** the clips play in reverse and a foot lands on its toes, heel up, so its ankle is higher when it touches the ground (0.24 to 0.29 m walking and up to 0.38 m running, where forward it is 0.19 to 0.22): `Backward Contact Height` (0.40) is the limit going straight back, and the limit goes from `Contact Height` to it as the body turns from forward or sideways to back. A step on the ankle's height alone (the first version) sounded about 160 ms before the foot: the ankle falls through any height in the middle of the swing. The sounds are synthesized by `tools/sounds/locomotion.py` (replace a file, keep its name).
- **The flash of a shot** (`MuzzleFlash`, next to each `Firearm`): the engine's particles, a spray of sparks along the barrel and a round glow at its tip, made when the weapon is first taken in hand; `Sparks`, `Spark Speed`, `Spark Spread` and `Glow Size` are its fields (the shotgun has more and bigger). The `Muzzle` entity keeps its light, which comes on for `Flash Time`. Particles are drawn by the main camera only, so the flash is on the default layer, behind the weapon (the arms camera draws the weapon).
- **The stamina of the run** (`Stamina`, `scripts/player/`, optional, not on the player in the scene: Add Component > Stamina on the player, Bravo's and Brava's; without it, or unticked, the `run` control runs for as long as it is held): running spends it, `Run Seconds` (10) from full to nothing, and anything else gives it back, `Recover Seconds` (7) from nothing to full. When it runs out the player cannot run until the stamina is back to `Resume At` (30 %), and then `run` is pressed again (a held control does not start the run by itself); a player who stops before it runs out can run again at once. The HUD shows a small bar at the bottom centre of the screen from the first step of a run until the stamina is full again (`Show Bar`), red and with a mark at `Resume At` while the player is out of breath. It takes the run away with the Player Movement's `canRun` (a script can do the same), and what it spends on is `isSprinting` (the run on, the keys moving the body, not crouched). The value and the out-of-breath state are in a save.
- **The pause menu** (`PauseMenu` service + the panel in `ui/`): when the player lets go of the pointer (Esc) after having held it, the game pauses (`Pauses Game`; Esc again, the Resume button or a click on the game closes it) and a panel offers Mouse Sensitivity (a multiple of the camera's own), Field of View and Volume (the master fader, for that run) the saves (below) and a Resume button; clicking anywhere on the game holds the pointer again and closes it. **Esc lets go of the pointer** (the browser does it, in a window and in fullscreen: the user must always be able to), and that opens the menu; the next Esc closes it, gives the game back and (in a build) takes the pointer again with `Cursor.lockAfterEscape()`: not inside the key (the browser is still handling that Esc as the one that lets go of the pointer, and gives the pointer and drops it at once), but after the key is up, watching that it holds and asking again, up to three times (a pointer let go within 0.4 s of being taken is not the player pausing). A browser that will not give a pointer that no click made is answered by the next key that is not Esc, or the next click, which holds it (a key is a gesture, Esc is not); and with the pointer free and the menu closed, Esc opens the menu. The Journal's Esc does the same. **In the editor's Play, in a window, Shift+Esc lets go of the pointer for the editor and the game goes on**: no menu, no pause (`Cursor.releasedForEditor`), and a click on the game holds the pointer again; a plain Esc is still the pause menu, so what is tested is what ships, and a build or fullscreen never does this. In the browser's own fullscreen (the editor's "Fullscreen on Play"; the runtime puts the page in it at the first click of a fullscreen window) the engine also asks the browser to hand Esc to the page (Keyboard Lock, `Cursor.takesEscapeInFullscreen`): a short Esc then does not leave fullscreen, and when it arrives with the pointer still held the game lets go of it itself, which is a release the page made, so it can take the pointer back with no click. To see what a browser does at an Esc, `BraveCoreModule.Cursor.debugEscape = true` in the console prints every Esc, every change of the pointer and every request for it. The settings are the game's own, kept in the Vault (`settings.*`) and applied at the start and at every change. Resume closes the menu even if the browser refuses the pointer, so the player is never trapped. **Closing a menu hands the pointer back only in a build** (`PauseMenu.holdPointer()`, which the Journal uses too): in the editor's Play the pointer stays free after Resume, Esc or Tab, so the developer can take a screenshot or use the editor with the game running and no menu in front; the next click on the game holds it, and Esc with the pointer free opens the menu again. The settings include **Controls** (a button that takes the panel's place: the Controls screen, below). Under Resume are **Restart** and **Quit**, each asking twice (the first click turns the button into "Click again to confirm" for three seconds; a click elsewhere, the other button or the menu closing takes it back). Restart puts the game back as it was on its first frame (the `SaveGame` service keeps that print in memory and restores it as a load does: scenes, Inventory, Journal and waves at their start), and the player's settings and saves stay as they are. Quit stops Play in the editor, quits the app in an installed build, and is not shown in a browser tab (`PauseMenu` `canQuit`).
- **Saving the game** (`SaveGame` service + the Saves block of the pause menu): the `quick-save` action (**F5**) saves to the quick slot and `quick-load` (**F9**) loads it (only while the game runs; a pad has neither by default, its player uses the menu: **Start** opens it without the pointer ever having been held, and the menu itself wants the mouse or the keyboard, there is no pad cursor). F5 never reloads the game, wherever the player puts quick save: `SaveGame` stops the browser's reload on its own (the reload is the browser's, not an action), and the pause menu has three slots more (Slot 1 to 3), each with the time it was saved and the health the player had, a Save and a Load button (Load is off while a slot is empty), and a line that says what the last save or load did. The work is the engine's `SaveSystem` (a print of the whole game: the scenes, the services that keep state, the Director); a load builds the saved game, drops the one that was running, puts the player's own settings (sensitivity, field of view) on the new camera and closes the menu. What the game keeps is whatever its scripts keep in fields (`@Saved()` for a runtime-only one, `onRestore()` for what a field cannot carry: a listener, a timer), the `Inventory` and the `Journal`; the saves live in the Vault of this game (its package name), never shared with another. To keep one more thing, give its script a field or its service `onSave()` / `onRestore(data)`; to change the slots, edit `SLOTS` in `services/save-game.service.ts`. Restart is a load of the game's first frame, kept in memory at the start (about the size of a save of the level).
- **A new hit zone**: a child entity with a solid Collider (not a trigger: a ray goes through triggers) and a `Hitbox` with the zone name and the multiplier.

- **Footsteps by surface**: the player steps with the sounds of the ground it stands on, chosen at each step by one ray down. ## The first-person rig, and what not to touch
The body is the character's own, animated by its own clips (`characters/*/..-clips`); the arms are driven by Two Bone IK to the weapon, which is a child of the right hand, and the camera rides the head. `FirstPersonArms` moves the arms to the weapon and back (its `Equip Key` is off here: the `Weapon` calls `equip()` and `unequip()` for the slot actions 1 to 9), `WeaponRetreat` pulls the weapon out of a wall, `WeaponStance` moves `Hold Right` (and `Grip Left`) for the two stances (the alert one carries the weapon low and to the right with the barrel turned in and raised, `Alert Side` / `Alert Lower` / `Alert Turn` / `Alert Tilt`, so it is seen from the side and its line goes to the middle of the view, the vanishing point, and not from behind; aiming takes those four back to nothing, and so does `Weapon Retreat` as it backs the weapon off a wall, so the retreat works from the pose it was made for; `Relaxed Pose` is the hip). One place writes each target: do not add a second script or constraint that writes `Hold Right`, or the two will fight (`docs/characters-and-animation.md`). **The arms camera rides the chest, not the head:** `Camera Hands` hangs from the Eyes in the scene, but `FirstPersonArms` (`Camera On Chest`, on) gives it a `Parent Constraint` (Position only, Source the `Lean Bone`) half a second into the game, once the body is standing on the ground with the look not far from level (two seconds in for a player who never stops), so the arms and the weapon look the same at every pitch and looking up never puts the camera inside the arms (until then it hangs from the Eyes, as authored), and puts its place right the first time the body has rested for a second with the look level: the camera then moves a centimetre or two, once, and only if the first place was further off than that; its rotation stays the Eyes', so it looks where the player looks. The weapon sways less on the screen as the body walks, and the muzzle's glow and sparks (world particles, drawn by the world camera: the arms camera draws none) sit a little off the barrel, more when looking far up or down. The two cameras are not in the same place (the arms camera is 0.2 to 0.3 m behind the Eyes, and on the chest it is further still when looking down), so the frame the arms change from the world camera's layer to the arms camera's would show them jump: a second `Parent Constraint` on `Camera Hands` (Source the Eyes, Position only, after the chest's) puts the camera exactly on the world camera while the arms are the body's (weight 1) and lets it go as they reach the weapon (weight 0 at `Equip Time`), both ways, so the layer changes on a picture that is the same from both cameras. Untick `Camera On Chest` to hang the camera from the Eyes again (and the swap shows).

## Traps
- A script that listens to an action must check that the game is running (`BraveEngine.mode`): `Input` callbacks fire during Pause. The click that locks the pointer must not shoot: `Loadout.holdsPointer()` says whether the mouse's controls count (the pointer is held, or the player is on a pad, which never needed it, while the game runs with no menu open: a menu that does not pause the game keeps a pad's shots out as the free pointer keeps a mouse's; `Fire Without Pointer Lock` is for tests).
- **No script of the kit reads a key.** A script that asks for `Input.keyboard.keyE` is that key for ever and the player cannot move it: ask for an action (Controls, above). A page of `ui/` that names a key draws it from the action (`data-input-action`), never as a letter written in the HTML.
- After a load, private handles filled in `onStart()` are `undefined`: the kit's scripts bind again in `onRestore()` (read `Interactor`, `Respawn` or `Weapon`), and a countdown that must survive is a saved field, not an `Invoke`.
- The shader of something that appears for the first time compiles in the background: the first frames of a new weapon or light may show nothing yet.
