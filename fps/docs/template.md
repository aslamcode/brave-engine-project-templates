# The FPS kit

A first-person shooter to start from, in a project that is yours to change: a yard and a house of two rooms (`scenes/main`), Bravo (1.8 m) or Brava (1.7 m) in first person with a rifle and a shotgun, interaction with E (doors, a drawer, pickups, a socket), cube enemies and waves, and a HUD. Everything is plain scripts and prefabs: read them, change them, delete what you do not want. Read `AGENTS.md` and the topic files in `docs/` for how the engine works; this file is about what this template made of it.

## Play it
- **WASD** move, **Shift** run, **Ctrl** crouch, **Space** jump, **mouse** look (click once to lock the pointer), **left mouse** fire, **right mouse** aim (hold), **R** reload, **E** use what the crosshair is on, **1** to **9** take the gear of that slot in hand (the key of the one in hand puts it away, again takes it out), the **mouse wheel** goes through the weapons.
- Walk into the house: the front door opens with E; the inner door is locked, its key is in the drawer of the cabinet in the first room; the power cell on the table goes into the socket in the back room (it switches the lamp on). The amber pad in the yard starts the waves.
- The weapon is carried **relaxed** (low) until you shoot, aim or reload, then **alert** (at the shoulder) and it goes back to relaxed after `Alert Seconds` (30) without a shot (at once when the weapon is put away, or a grenade or a medkit is in hand); running lowers it too. That is how the character holds it. **Aiming** (right mouse held) is something more: the weapon comes to the eye, the field of view narrows and the spread closes; a sprint, a reload or a change of weapon lets it go.
- Four things in hand: the **rifle** (slot 1: automatic, 30 rounds), the **shotgun** (slot 2: ten pellets a shot, one shot at a time, six shells, a pump sound), a **grenade** (slot 3: the left mouse throws it) and a **medkit** (slot 4: the left mouse held heals). Each firearm keeps its own rounds when another is in hand; the grenades and the medkits are counted by the Inventory, and two of the first and one of the second are on the ground near the start.

## How the parts talk
- **The `Hud` service** (`services/hud.service.ts`) is the only way the game puts something on the screen: the prompt line, ammo, health, stance, a message, the items, a hit marker, which side a blow came from (`notifyDamaged(amount, angle)`: a red arc on that side of the crosshair), a crosshair that turns red over a living target (`setTarget`: the `Weapon` asks every frame with the same ray a shot uses, and a weapon with several pellets also along a ring inside its cone, so red means a shot now could hurt it) and markers over the things that can be used (`setMarkers`: the `Interactor` projects them to the screen). Scripts `@Inject(Hud)` and call `setPrompt`, `setAmmo`, `showMessage`...; `ui/hud.js` reads it through `remote('Hud')`. Change the page to change the look; add a field to `IHudState` to show something new.
- **The `Inventory` service** keeps what the player carries by item id (`'key'`, `'power-cell'`, `'credits'`, and the rounds: `'ammo-rifle'`, `'ammo-shotgun'`). A `Pickup` adds, a `Socket` removes, a locked `Door` asks (and takes the key out when it opens, unless its `Use Up Key` is off: the Journal still lists it, as used), a `Firearm` draws its reserve from its `Ammo Type`. A kind may have a **limit** (the most that can be carried; 0 or none is no limit, as keys or credits have) and may be **hidden** from the list the HUD shows (the rounds are in the ammo counter, not in the list). Whoever knows a kind says what it is with `define(id, { name, limit, hidden, start })`: a weapon for its rounds, a `Pickup` (its `Limit`) for its item; `add()` stops at the limit and `room(id)` says how many more fit. A `Pickup` that finds the player full stays where it is, and one that fits only some gives what fits. It tells the `Hud` and it is saved with the game.
- **The player's own pieces, by job**: `Health` (the number), `DamageFeedback` (the health bar, the side a blow came from, the hurt cry: it only listens to the `Health`), `Respawn` (what happens at zero: the message, the seconds, back to the start with everything full again: a game with lives changes `respawn()` only), `Loadout` (what is in hand) and `Weapon` (the firearm). The player's sounds are all below its `Sounds` entity: the shots, the reloads, the hurt cry and `Footstep Sounds`.
- **`Health` and `Hitbox`**: a shot hurts the `Health` it finds above the collider it hit, by damage times the multiplier of the `Hitbox` on that collider (head 2.5, body 1...). Anything can have a `Health`: the player, an enemy, a crate.
- **The Director** can drive the interactables: `Door`, `Socket` and `Pickup` have `@State()` booleans and `@Callable()` methods (`docs/director.md`).

## Make it yours
- **The Journal** (`Journal` service, `JournalMenu`, `Collectible`, `Objective`, and its page in `ui/`): what the player has found out, to look at again whenever it likes, with **Tab**: Objectives, Access (keys and cards, and whether they were used), Passwords, Notes and Audio Logs. It is not what the player uses (that is the Inventory's: a key is an item there, and an entry here) and it has no ammo. It takes the whole screen (the pause menu is never seen under it). The chip in the corner of the screen (the key and the menu's name) is not always there: it fades in when something new goes into the menu and out again after `Hint Seconds` (5; 0 keeps it on), set on the `JournalMenu`. The menu has the categories on the left, the entries of one in the middle with a drawing for the chosen one, and its text on the right (the note to read, or a play button for an audio log, which goes on playing while the game is paused because it does not go through an Audio Source). Tab opens and closes it (Esc closes it too), the pointer is let go while it is open and held again when it closes (Esc is not a gesture to a browser, so after it the next click holds the pointer). A `JournalMenu` in the scene says what the menu is called (`Title`: Journal here; Journal, Datapad, Notebook or Terminal in another game, with nothing else changing) and whether it pauses the game (`Pauses Game`, on). A `Collectible` is a thing to take (`Category`: notes, audio logs or passwords; `Title`, `Text`, `Audio Log`, `Open On Take`); a `Pickup` with `Journal Category` `access` lists its item under Access and the `Door` or the `Socket` that uses it marks it used; an `Objective` is a goal that a `Socket` completes (`Completes Objective`) or a script (`give()`, `complete()`, `fail()`). The yard has a torn page near the start, the house an audio log and a password and the objective of the power. A script uses the service: `journal.add({ id, category, title, body })`. `tools/fps-kit/add-journal.py` gives a project the same.
- **A weapon** is an entity under `Weapons` in the right hand (a child of the hand bone) with a `Firearm`: its numbers are the fields of that `Firearm`: `Name`, `Slot` (the key that takes it in hand), `Automatic`, `Rounds per Minute`, `Pellets` (bullets in a shot: 1 a rifle, 8 to 12 a shotgun, each in a direction of its own inside the cone), `Damage` (of each), `Range`, the spread `Still / Moving / In the Air`, the `Crosshair` (Dot, Arcs: a ring of four arcs round a dot, or Twin Arcs, the shotgun's: two arcs one each side of a dot; all open with the spread, so the arcs show where the pellets may go), `Ammo Type` (the Inventory item it fires: weapons of one type share the rounds), `Magazine`, `Reserve` (what the player starts with, once per game and again after a respawn), `Reserve Limit` (the most of that type the player can carry; 0 is no limit), `Reload Time`, `Camera Kick`, and the `Aim` group (`Aim Field of View`, `Aim Time`, `Aim Spread`, and `Aim Side / Lower / Back / Tilt`: how far the weapon moves and how far its barrel is raised to end on the line of the eye, a little under it). Its parts are children: the model, `Muzzle` (a light and a flash, off) and `Grip Left` (where the left hand goes), and the sounds are references to entities under the player's `Sounds`. The `Weapon` on the player (one for all of them) has `Auto Reload` (on: a magazine that runs empty is reloaded by itself, once the shot is over, when there are rounds in reserve; a weapon taken in hand empty or given rounds while empty does the same; off: R reloads and the trigger on an empty magazine clicks). The two stances' poses are `WeaponStance` fields (`Lower`, `Back`, `Side`, `Tilt`...). The rifle and the shotgun are placeholders made of cubes: put a model in place of the cubes (keep `Muzzle` at the barrel's tip and `Grip Left` where the left hand goes).
- **The `Loadout` and the gear**: the `Loadout` on the player is what the player carries in hand: the entity in the right hand (`Gear`, the `Weapons` entity) with a `Gear` on each child (a `Firearm`, a `Throwable`, a `Medkit`...). It takes one in hand with the keys **1** to **9** (a gear's `Slot`) and the wheel, puts it away with the key of the one in hand, lists them on the HUD, points the left hand at the grip of the one in hand, and passes the left mouse, the right mouse and **R** on to it (the gear's hooks `onPrimaryDown()`, `onSecondaryDown()`, `onReload()`..., and the signals `onPrimaryDown`, `onSecondaryDown`, `onReload`, `onSwitch` for anything else that listens: the `Weapon` fires a `Firearm` from them). `Start Equipped` (off: the first gear starts put away, loaded, and its key brings it up) and `Fire Without Pointer Lock` (a test's) are its fields. `Weapon` is only for firearms: with a grenade in hand it does nothing.
- **The grenade** (`Throwable`, slot 3): the left mouse winds up (`Wind-up`) and throws; one is taken off the count, the `Projectile` prefab (`prefabs/gear/grenade.fab`: a Rigid Body, a Collider and a `Grenade`) is made in front of the eyes and sent where the player looks, `Lift` degrees above it, at `Throw Speed` plus the body's own. The `Grenade` goes off after `Fuse` seconds: the engine's Small explosion, and every `Health` inside `Radius` takes `Damage` falling off with the distance (a wall between the blast and a body protects it); a rigid body is pushed. The count is the Inventory's (`Item Id` `grenade`, `Start With`, `Limit`): a `Pickup` of that id gives more, and the list on the HUD says how many are left (`Grenade ×2`).
- **The medkit** (`Medkit`, slot 4): the left mouse HELD heals `Amount` over `Use Time` and spends one (`Item Id` `medkit`); letting go, taking another gear or dying stops it with nothing spent, and a player at full health is told so. Placeholders of boxes like the rifle: put your own models in, keep `Grip Left` where the left hand goes.
- **A new kind of gear** (a torch, a flare): a script that `extends Gear` (`label`, `slot`, `gripLeft`) and overrides the hooks it uses: `onPrimaryDown()`, `onPrimaryUp()`, `onSecondaryDown()`, `onSecondaryUp()`, `onReload()`, `onBrought()`, `onPutAway()`, `showHud()` (what the HUD says of it), `prepare()` (tell the Inventory what its items are), `refill()` (the player is back from the dead) and `caption` (what the list says). It sits on an entity under `Weapons`, so it is on only while it is in hand: its `onUpdate()` runs only then.
- **A new weapon**: copy the `Shotgun` entity under `Weapons` (or the `Rifle`), give its `Firearm` the next free `Slot`, other numbers and another model; move its `Grip Left` and its `Muzzle`. The key, the wheel, the HUD list and the left hand's reach find it by themselves (it is whatever is under `Weapons` with a `Gear`). The model sits in the hand where you put the entity: the right hand holds the pistol grip at the entity's origin.
- **A new thing to use with E**: a script that `extends Interactable` (`prompt`, `usable`, `onUse(interactor)`), on an entity with a collider; `Door`, `Pickup` and `Socket` are the examples. Its `Marker` (Line: a glass box with the prompt over it, tied to it by a line; Simple: the box on it; None) and `Marker Height` say how it is shown on the screen, and the `Interactor`'s `Marker Range` and `Max Markers` say how many are drawn: the one looked at is the box, the near ones are a small mark. A door that slides (a drawer, a shutter) is the same `Door` with `Mode` Slide.
- **A new enemy**: copy `prefabs/enemies/enemy-cube.fab`: its `Health`, its `Hitbox` children (a head, a body), `EnemyCube` with `Aggro Range`, `Speed`, `Attack Damage`; give the `WaveSpawner` the prefab. A wave can mix a second kind in: `Second Prefab` (the humanoid) and `Second Share` (0.35: about a third of each wave); the yard's and the combat demo's pads do.
- **A supply** (an ammo box, a first aid kit): an `Interactable`, so it is marked like a door or a pickup (the glass box with E and what it gives, tied to it by a line, when it is looked at) and taken with **E**. The entity has a trigger Collider (the space round it, for `Walk Over`) and an `AmmoSupply` (`Rounds`, `Ammo Type`: the item it puts in the Inventory, the `Ammo Type` of the weapons that fire it) or a `HealthSupply` (`Amount`), its drawing as children with a solid Collider on `Visual` (what the player's ray hits: a ray goes through triggers), `Visual` pointing at it (hidden while it is used up), an Audio Source on the entity for the sound, `Prompt` (`{amount}` is what it gives), `Message`, `Full Message` (what E says when the weapon or the player has no use for it: `Ammo full`, `Health full`), `Respawn After` (0: taken for good) and `Walk Over` (on: it is also taken by walking into the trigger). The player carries no more rounds of a type than its `Reserve Limit`. To make another kind (a key card, a grenade), extend `Supply` and write `give(player)` (and `promptAmount()` for the prompt): what it returns is how much was given, 0 leaves it where it is. The yard has an ammo box and a first aid kit near where the player starts.
- **The humanoid enemy** (`prefabs/enemies/enemy-humanoid.fab`, two in the yard and two in the combat demo): Bravo with the red materials (`bravo-hostile.mat`), the player's own walking and running clips (its `Animator` and `Character Animation` on the root, told by the way the `Nav Agent` moves it), and the same `EnemyCube` mind as the cube: it hunts, hits and falls the same way. A shot meets a hit zone on every bone that matters (head 2.5x, chest and belly 1x, pelvis 0.9x, arms 0.5 to 0.6x, legs 0.6 to 0.7x), each a `Hitbox` with **Follow Animation** on (a collider is placed once, so it is put where its bone is at every frame: `Collider.syncToEntity()`). Its capsule only walks: it is on the layer `Enemy Shell`, which the weapon's rays pass, so that a shot at the head is a head and not the capsule. To make another humanoid, copy the prefab and change the materials; a different skeleton needs the zones on its own bones.
- **Footsteps** (`Footsteps`, `scripts/locomotion/`, on the player and on the humanoid): one of four step sounds plays for every foot that comes down: at the point where the foot gets farthest ahead of the body along the way it goes (a heel strike, read from the ankle bones frame by frame, so any clip works: walk, run, crouch, sidestep, and no clip needs an animation event), louder with the speed (`Volume`, `Run Speed`, `Walk Share`), softer crouched (`Crouch Share`), silent standing, turning on the spot or in the air, and `Land Sound` when the body lands from a fall. The player's sounds are flat, the humanoid's come from where it is. The variations of a holder play in the ORDER of its children (drag them in the Hierarchy to change it) and start again (`Step Order`: Sequence, the default; or Random, which never repeats the one that just played): a plain random choice plays the same sound twice in a row now and then, and that is what makes a walk sound like a machine. Each surface has its own place in its sequence. `Contact Height` is measured from the entity's origin (a level with steps may need a higher one). A step on the ankle's height alone (the first version) sounded about 160 ms before the foot: the ankle falls through any height in the middle of the swing. The sounds are synthesized by `tools/sounds/locomotion.py` (replace a file, keep its name).
- **The flash of a shot** (`MuzzleFlash`, next to each `Firearm`): the engine's particles, a spray of sparks along the barrel and a round glow at its tip, made when the weapon is first taken in hand; `Sparks`, `Spark Speed`, `Spark Spread` and `Glow Size` are its fields (the shotgun has more and bigger). The `Muzzle` entity keeps its light, which comes on for `Flash Time`. Particles are drawn by the main camera only, so the flash is on the default layer, behind the weapon (the arms camera draws the weapon).
- **The pause menu** (`PauseMenu` service + the panel in `ui/`): when the player lets go of the pointer (Esc) after having held it, the game pauses (`Pauses Game`; Esc again, the Resume button or a click on the game closes it) and a panel offers Mouse Sensitivity (a multiple of the camera's own), Field of View and Volume (the master fader, for that run) and a Resume button; clicking anywhere on the game holds the pointer again and closes it. The settings are the game's own, kept in the Vault (`settings.*`) and applied at the start and at every change. Resume closes the menu even if the browser refuses the pointer, so the player is never trapped.
- **A new hit zone**: a child entity with a solid Collider (not a trigger: a ray goes through triggers) and a `Hitbox` with the zone name and the multiplier.
- **Sounds**: replace a `.wav` under `assets/audio/` (same name keeps every reference) or edit `tools/sounds/*.py` and run it. Credits are in `NOTICE.md`.
- **Marks of the shots**: a bullet that hits something that does not move leaves a 7 cm stain on it (`DecalSystem.stain`, the oil look); a body or an enemy gets none. The stains of a game (blood, oil, scorch, these) share ONE ring of **Max Stains** (`quality.maxStains` in `project.json`, Project Settings, Quality, Decals; the default, 1024, which this kit keeps): when it is full the newest takes the slot of the oldest, and the oldest tenth fades out before it goes, so nothing piles up for ever. At most 256 stains are drawn in a frame (the nearest ones), and 1024 holds about 100 s of rifle fire, so an old mark goes long after anybody looked at it; a much lower number would show the marks vanishing while you look at them.
- **Footsteps by surface**: the player steps with the sounds of the ground it stands on, chosen by the PHYSICS MATERIAL of the collider under the foot (one ray down at each step). A `Footstep Surface` is an entity with a physics material (a menu of the project's `.pmat` assets), the entity that holds its walk sounds and the one that holds its run sounds (an Audio Source for each variation, as `Footsteps` takes its own); the surfaces live together under `Footstep Surfaces` in the scene, and a `Footsteps` with **By Surface** on (the players have it) takes the sounds of the surface that has the floor's physics material, or its own `Step Sound` when the floor has none (a collider with no physics material, or one nobody made a surface for). A new kind of ground is a physics material, a floor that has it and one more `Footstep Surface`. The kit has twenty, from `base-project`'s footstep folders (`audio/footsteps/<surface>`, a `walk/` and a `run/` where the sounds differ), each with a physics material (the ones `base-project` has, and new ones whose numbers are only plausible) and a floor in the `main` scene, **behind the player's start** (z 31 to 37, two rows of ten, 4 m a side, 6 cm high), painted a colour that says nothing about the ground. - **A humanoid that dies falls as a ragdoll**: the `Enemy Humanoid` has the engine's `Ragdoll` (the Bravo's parts: it is the same model, with the same bones), and `EnemyCube` goes limp with it when its `Health` runs out, with the blow of the shot that killed it (`Death Push`, in newton seconds, along the shot at the point it hit; 0 lets it fall where it stands). It holds the animation itself, the body's own colliders and the hit zones are switched off (the dead are not shot again), and it lies `Corpse Seconds` (5) before it is removed; the wave is not cleared while it lies there. A game with one character keeps the one it uses and deletes the other (the one that is off costs nothing but its place in the file).

## The first-person rig, and what not to touch
The body is the character's own, animated by its own clips (`characters/*/..-clips`); the arms are driven by Two Bone IK to the weapon, which is a child of the right hand, and the camera rides the head. `FirstPersonArms` moves the arms to the weapon and back (its `Equip Key` is off here: the `Weapon` calls `equip()` and `unequip()` for keys 1 to 9), `WeaponRetreat` pulls the weapon out of a wall, `WeaponStance` moves `Hold Right` (and `Grip Left`) for the two stances. One place writes each target: do not add a second script or constraint that writes `Hold Right`, or the two will fight (`docs/characters-and-animation.md`).

## Traps
- A script that listens to the mouse or a key must check that the game is running (`BraveEngine.mode`): `Input` callbacks fire during Pause. The click that locks the pointer must not shoot: `Weapon` checks `Cursor.isLocked` (`Fire Without Pointer Lock` is for tests).
- After a load, private handles filled in `onStart()` are `undefined`: the kit's scripts bind again in `onRestore()` (read `Interactor`, `Respawn` or `Weapon`), and a countdown that must survive is a saved field, not an `Invoke`.
- The shader of something that appears for the first time compiles in the background: the first frames of a new weapon or light may show nothing yet.

## Not built yet (ideas the kit is ready for)
- Bullets that go through things by material and thickness, and a ballistic arc: `Weapon.fireBullet()` casts one ray (each pellet of a shotgun is one) and `onBulletHit()` returns the damage a bullet still has (0 today): a loop there. The engine's rays do not continue through a collider, a physics material has no surface type, and there is no overlap query yet (an explosion's damage would need one).
- Animations for the humanoid enemy that are its own (an attack, a flinch when it is hit: Mixamo clips go in `mixamo/`, which is not committed).
- Area damage (grenades), a real rifle model and a muzzle flash that is not a square.
